When I started my HR Attrition project, I expected pay to be the main story. It turned out overtime was the clearest single driver: people working overtime left at 30.5%, against 10.4% for everyone else.

## Starting with requirements, not charts

I treated the project the way I'd run one at work. I wrote a Business Requirements Document and user stories before building anything, so every page of the report answers one question a business leader would actually ask.

## What the data showed

- Overtime staff made up 54% of all leavers.
- Job Level 1 accounted for 143 of the 237 leavers.
- Overtime combined with the lowest pay band pushed attrition to 58.5%.
