About

Process first, data next

I'm a Business Process Analyst at African Bank in Midrand, working in Business & Commercial Banking. I joined as a Business Analyst in March 2025 and was promoted into process work. I look at how people, processes, systems and data fit together across the client lifecycle, then design and document a better way of working that stays within the bank's regulatory controls.

My path here runs through software development, data capture, operations analytics at Bolt and business analysis at Sasfin Bank. I have a BSc in Computer Science and Informatics from the University of Johannesburg and an Honours in IT from Richfield, and I'm now building toward BI and data engineering roles.

Discover

Gather requirements and map the current state

Interviews and workshops with the people doing the work, to capture what the business needs, who does what, in which system, and where work waits or loops back.

Design

Model the target process

BPMN models placed in the value stream, with the controls a regulated bank needs built in from the start.

Document

Write the SOP

Procedures with decision rules, system steps and the role responsible for each step, written so they can be followed on day one.

Validate

Test and sign off

UAT scenarios traced back to the requirements, so the business can confirm the new process and system changes do what was agreed.

A six-page Power BI report showing where a consumer goods business makes and loses money, built on 18,240 order lines across 17 countries and three years.

HR Attrition Analytics

A three-page Power BI report on why 237 of 1,470 employees left, built from a BRD and user stories through to a validated star schema, the way I'd run a project at work.

  • Overtime is the strongest driver: 30.5% attrition against 10.4%, and 54% of all leavers
  • Entry level carries the risk: Job Level 1 accounts for 143 of the 237 leavers
  • Overtime plus the lowest pay band pushes attrition to 58.5%

Analyst lensStarted with a Business Requirements Document and user stories, followed a 10-stage analytics life cycle, and ended with recommendations for HR leadership.
